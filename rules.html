How to Play

A movie-rating guessing game for 2 or more players. Higher, lower, or right on the nose. Last correct guess wins.

01 · Cast & Crew

Setup

  • Players: 2 or more
  • Takes: 3 each (what we call lives — film slang)
  • You'll need: IMDb access on one phone or laptop

Two designated roles, fixed for the entire game (no rotation, no "left of the guesser"):

★ Role 01
The Scorekeeper

Runs the tracker. Marks takes lost, takes regained, and correct guesses. Holds the rulebook.

★ Role 02
The Searcher

Looks up films on IMDb and reads the rating aloud. Cannot coach, cannot hint, cannot raise an eyebrow.

Both still play. They just take on the off-screen duties.

02 · The Loop

The Core Loop

  • Player 1 names a film. The Searcher pulls it up on IMDb and announces the rating. This is the anchor.
  • The next player names a film — without looking it up — trying to land per the chosen mode and threshold.
  • The Searcher reveals the rating. That film becomes the new anchor — whether the guess landed or failed.
  • One guess per turn, no retries. A correct guess survives; a wrong guess costs a take. Either way, play passes immediately to the next player, who now faces the new anchor.
★ Important

Losing a take ends your turn. You don't get a second attempt at the same anchor. The next player is up, working off the new (failed) anchor.

03 · Modes

Modes

Agree on one before starting.

Ascending

Every guess must rate higher than the anchor.

Descending

Every guess must rate lower than the anchor.

Switchback

Direction flips at an agreed ceiling and floor — defaults are 8.5 / 6.0, but the group sets both before play. Pinballs between IMDb's peaks and valleys.

Drift

No direction set. The second player's guess locks the direction until the next take is lost. After a loss, the next guesser sets a fresh direction.

In Frame ★ NEW

Direction doesn't matter — but the guess must stay within the threshold of the anchor, up or down. Step out of frame, lose a take. Requires one of the named thresholds below.

05 · Comeback

Meets It, Beats It

An exact tie with the anchor is not a failure — it's a clutch. The guess clears the round; the anchor stays the same number going into the next turn.

★ Comeback Rule

An exact tie also regains a take, up to the firm cap of 3. You cannot exceed 3 takes at any point — extra ties past full health are just clutch guesses.

06 · Penalties

You Lose a Take If…

  • Your film goes the wrong way in a directional mode (e.g., aiming higher, anchor was 7.8, you land 6.9).
  • Your film busts the threshold when one is in play.
  • Your film falls out of frame in In Frame mode.
  • The film isn't on IMDb or has fewer than 1,000 votes.
  • You repeat a film already played this game.
  • You break a franchise rule (see below).
  • You can't name a film within 30 seconds.

A player at zero takes is out. The winner is whoever had the last correct guess when the game ends.

07 · Franchises

Repeats & Franchises

  • No repeating films. Once a film is played, it's burned for the rest of the game.
  • Two-per-franchise cap. Each franchise can contribute a maximum of two films per game. A third pick from the same franchise costs a take.
  • No back-to-back franchise picks. The very next guess after a franchise pick cannot come from the same franchise. One turn of breathing room minimum.
  • What counts as a franchise: numbered sequels and prequels, direct continuations, and acknowledged shared cinematic universes (MCU, DCEU, Wizarding World, Star Wars saga, Middle-earth, etc.). Reboots from a different studio or canon (e.g., the 2002 Spider-Man vs the MCU one) count as the same franchise — same character, same franchise.
  • What doesn't count: loose genre or stylistic similarity. Heist movies aren't a franchise. Movies by the same director aren't a franchise.
  • Disputes: if the table can't agree whether two films share a franchise, majority vote decides. Ties go in favor of the guesser.
08 · Timer

Game Timer

Optional. For a punchier session, set a fixed game length — 5, 10, 15, or 30 minutes.

When the buzzer goes, play stops on the current turn and the last correct guess wins. Best paired with Razor's Edge thresholds for a chaotic blitz.

09 · Fine Print

Edge Cases

  • Eligible films: feature-length (40 min+), on IMDb with 1,000+ votes. No shorts. TV movies and miniseries are out unless the whole table agrees up front.
  • Ratings are final at the moment of lookup, even if IMDb updates a minute later.
  • The Searcher reads the number, nothing else. No coaching, no hints, no eyebrow movements.
  • Drift mode tie-on-second-guess: if P2 ties P1's anchor, no direction is set yet — P3 plays freely against the same anchor and their result sets the direction.
  • Switchback mid-turn flip: the flip applies to the next guesser, not retroactively. If you push the anchor over the agreed ceiling, you survive; the next player now goes the other way.
  • In Frame with no threshold isn't a game. Pick one of the named thresholds before starting.
10 · Victory

Winning

The winner is whoever had the last correct guess when the game ends.

The game ends when only one player has takes remaining, or when the optional Game Timer runs out.

In standard play, the last player standing is automatically the most recent correct guesser, so the rule resolves itself. It mainly matters when the timer expires with multiple players still alive, or in the rare case of simultaneous elimination.

★ Pro Tip

The Scorekeeper should keep a running list of films played so no one accidentally repeats. Roll credits.
